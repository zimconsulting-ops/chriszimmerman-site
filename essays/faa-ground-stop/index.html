The FAA stopped every departure today

What the FAA has said so far, what is only reported, and what it means if you are flying out of RDU.

Christian Zimmerman · November 13, 2026 · updated 5:30 PM ET

This afternoon the FAA held every departing flight at every airport in the country. It is only the second time that has happened since 9/11, and the first was in January 2023, when the system pilots use for pre-flight notices went down.

What the FAA has said

The FAA says it ordered the stop for safety, under what it called extraordinary circumstances, and it points to communication problems at several of its Air Route Traffic Control Centers, which are the facilities that handle planes at cruising altitude between airports. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the problem reaches all of those centers and that FAA technicians are working on it. Planes already in the air are still flying, and international flights headed to the US are being handled case by case. Earlier in the afternoon, Chicago's O'Hare and Midway had their own stop for an equipment problem.

What is reported but not confirmed

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told reporters that, as of now, it is a telecommunications outage with no sign yet of an attack (ABC News), which is reassuring but is not a final finding on the cause. An internal Alaska Airlines message seen by CNBC describes radar problems as well as communications, and I have not seen anything that ties this to controller staffing. As I write this there is no word on when the stop will lift.

If you are flying out of RDU

The order covers every US airport, RDU included. Check your airline's app before you leave for the airport, and plan for disruptions that may continue after the stop lifts while airlines get their crews and planes back in place. The FAA posts live status at nasstatus.faa.gov.

A local note

This is the second time in about a month that a communications failure has stopped flights at RDU. On October 12, according to RDU officials as reported by WRAL, an FAA contractor cut a fiber-optic line near the tower during a plumbing repair, and flights there were held off and on until about 9 that night. I will come back to whether the two have anything in common once the FAA says what failed today.

Sources: CNBC and ABC News (Nov 13), WRAL (Oct 12), FAA National Airspace System status.
